# June 19, 2026

I haven't had much time to work on this this week, but I have been thinking about my current path of study, which is the maintenance or degredation of company culture. For example, when I read a profile of Menlo Industries written in 2014, it sounded idealistic in a way that only a small tech company in 2014 could be. It sounded like early 8th Light or Formidable. But I know from experience that neither of those cultures lasted, so even though Menlo Innovations has written about a lot as a model of what work could be, I'm skeptical of whether "The Menlo Way" has had any real impact on tech culture, or whether it's even upheld by their own employees internally.

In the book _Enshittification_, Cory Doctorow says that the more relevant question isn't "why do products enshittify", but "what prevents products from enshittification?" Becuase enshittificaion is a natural outcome of hypercapitalism, and it requires certain forces to hold it back. Products don't remain good because the company's leaders care, they remain good because they must compete in the market, employees have bargaining power and push back, or they are regulated.

I guess the question I want to ask here is "what prevents cultural enshittification?" Because it's rarely the fact that leaders care about the culture. There are usually specific market forces that preserve the culture for a period of time, and once those market forces erode, the culture starts to enshittify.

I think there was this millenial-era optimism where we thought that if we just had better intentions, if we trained people better, if we "cared" more, if we tracked DEI metrics, if we formed committees and took on the extra work of culture building, we would finally get it right. And maybe for a little while, we had enough power in the job market to hold back the tide a little bit. But once the forces holding back the enshittifiction eroded, or were dismantled, the progress was lost.

I want to better understand that story. What keeps good culture in place? Is that even possible in the face of capitalism?
