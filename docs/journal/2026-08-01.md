The question I kept thinking about when writing the masculine energy piece, is "what exactly is wrong with masculine-coded language"? After all, masculininty in and of itself isn't necessarily a bad thing. Building, moving decisively, communicating clarly are usually good things to do at work.

Same with the non-political workplace stance that many compnies are being public about. On its face, it's not a terrible thing. People have different view points, and the rules of professionalism sometimes require us to put these viewpoints aside. But the problem is that there is a pretty clear pipeline from masculine language to non-political stance to right-wing extremism.

Just look at DHH. Basecamp is the second most masculine-coded copmany in my corpus. In 2021, there was a very public announcement made about their new non-political stance and disbanding the DEI committee. And now he is posting about European migrants on his company blog.

I wouldn't have such an issue with the first two if they weren't so often a harbinger of more explicit political extremism.

I don't think I can say this in a scholarly way, but there's a right-wing register that many founders speak in where they probably think they are coming off as neutral and performance-driven, but actually they're making it clear that they want to protect a specific kind of many from criticism, but not protect marginalized employees, even if they are genuine high performers.

So here's my conclusion: I don't have a problem with masculine-coded language or political neutrality. I have an issue with right-wing extremism that dehumanizes people, and I wish these things weren't so tightly correlated.
