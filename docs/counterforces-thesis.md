# What holds a work culture in place — the counterforces thesis

The project's spine. Menlo, Automattic, and the DEI/altruism/performance cohort are all
instances of one claim:

> A workplace culture does not stay good because leaders care about it. It stays good
> while specific **counterforces** hold the default (market-driven decay) back. When
> those counterforces erode, the culture enshittifies regardless of stated intentions —
> and the **codified language persists as liturgy while the practice reverts.**

This follows Doctorow's inversion of enshittification: the question is not "why do
cultures decay?" (decay is the default) but "what *prevents* cultural decay — and what
happens to the language of values when that force is gone?"

---

## The 2010s millennial bet, and how it actually turned out

The 2010s carried real optimism that millennials would fix work — care more about
culture, enjoy the work, pressure leaders toward employee happiness, install DEI metrics
and committees. The honest 2026 reframing is **not** "millennials tried and failed." It
is: **the gains were real but rented, not owned.** They tracked *worker bargaining
power*, peaked when it peaked, and reverted when it inverted — not because anyone stopped
caring, but because the structural condition that made caring cheap evaporated.

### The arc is already in our data (numbers from the existing story exports)

- **Idealism rose then fell** (`altruism.json`). Amazon careers idealism: **peak +1.77
  (2013) → −0.01 (2026)** — a full collapse from "change the world" to neutral. Google
  held high; Meta cratered late. Millennial-era mission idealism peaked early-mid 2010s
  and decayed.
- **DEI surged then was rolled back** (`dei.json`). Industry-mean active-DEI share
  climbed to **0.14 (2022) → ~0.00 (2023)**, while counter-registers (meritocracy,
  "civilizational mission," apolitical-workplace) rose to **0.12 by 2025, overtaking
  active DEI.** The committees-and-metrics era dismantled, in the language itself.
- **Intensity never left and hardened** (`performance.json`). Performance language sat
  ~0.9 throughout; the *stance* hardened post-2020 — "not a family," keeper test,
  high-performer ultimatum, "generous severance." The thing the millennial dream reacted
  against was structurally dominant the whole time.

### Why it reverted (the counterforce timing)
The idealism / DEI / remote / "caring" peak (≈2014–2021) coincides with the tightest
tech labor market in a generation: war for talent, ZIRP-funded hypergrowth, the 2021
Great Resignation (record quit rates). The reversal (2022–2026) coincides with the
inversion: ~500k tech layoffs (2022–2024), RTO mandates, end of ZIRP, the AI overhang.
When workers couldn't walk, the language reverted.

**The bet's error:** that *intentions + programs* (DEI metrics, committees, culture work,
"happiness" as a value, codified Ways) could *lock in* good culture. They couldn't —
they were downstream of labor-market power, not a substitute for it. We built the
**symptom** (programs, statements, "the Menlo Way") instead of the **cause** (durable
worker power). When the market turned, the programs were cut first.

## How good workplaces are actually created and maintained

By **durable counterforces**, not intentions:

1. **Worker bargaining power** — tight labor market and/or organized labor (the only
   force workers control directly).
2. **Competition for talent and reputation** — when leaving is easy and reputation is
   visible.
3. **Owner/founder stake insulated from growth pressure** — Menlo (private, founder-led,
   small, not chasing hypergrowth) *kept* its culture because no counterforce was eroding
   — but for the same reason had no force to *export* it. Boutique, not a movement.
4. **Regulation** — the floor when the others fail.

Good culture is a *structural* outcome, not a moral one.

## Guardrail
Keep counterforces as **annotation, not a fitted causal claim**, until separately
pre-registered (plan §Phase 4). Honest statement: "the language reversal *co-occurs
with* the labor-market inversion," never "layoffs *caused* the reversal." The instrument
measures the symptom (canon drift, the canon↔practice gap); the counterforce is the
interpretive layer.

## Where each case sits
- **Cohort (Google/Amazon/Meta/…):** the rented gains — idealism/DEI rose and reverted
  with worker power.
- **Automattic:** weaponization — codified values turned outward (mark enforcement) once
  the asset was defensible.
- **Menlo:** the boutique exception — durable, flat idealism (every era mildly positive,
  never collapsing), kept internally because insulated, but never propagated (no named
  adopters; impact measured in tour visitors and book reach). Caring + broadcasting,
  absent a counterforce, changes one workplace, not an industry.
