# Wojcicki's version — the other witness to Google's first maternity leave

Companion to `parental-leave-history.md` (§1). Cairns and Wojcicki are the
two surviving tellings of the same 1999 event: the administrator's account
(2026 Threads/memoir) and the mother's account (told repeatedly, 2014–2017).
This doc assembles Wojcicki's version and reads the two against each other
and against the archive. Compiled 2026-07-16 from targeted searches; all
web-sourced, cite-checked to the listed sources but without a full
adversarial pass (unlike the main report).

## Her telling, assembled

- **1998:** Rents her Menlo Park garage to Page and Brin — she is Google's
  landlord before she is its employee.
- **1999:** Joins as ~employee #16 and first marketing manager, **four
  months pregnant**, at a ~15-person, revenue-less, almost-all-male company.
  Her line: "Joining Google when I was four months pregnant was a bit of a
  leap." She says the founders assured her of their support.
- **The ask:** "I'm going to take a little time off... Nobody had ever done
  it before at the company" (CBS interview). No policy existed. Her
  **personal request became the institutionalized 12-week policy** — her
  framing, repeated across tellings.
- **Late 1999:** first child; first Google maternity leave.
- **The milestone mapping:** she associated each of her five children with a
  corporate milestone — #1 with Google itself, #2 with AdSense ("we created
  AdSense after I came back from maternity leave"), #3 with the YouTube
  acquisition, #4 with DoubleClick. Maternity and product history fused into
  one narrative — the exact inverse of the leave-as-cost framing.
- **2007:** the 12→18-week expansion, which she publicized (and reportedly
  championed internally) with the 50%-attrition-drop statistic (see main
  report §2 for caveats: self-reported, never audited).
- **Dec 2014:** WSJ op-ed "Paid Maternity Leave Is Good for Business,"
  written while pregnant with her fifth child; as YouTube CEO she then took
  **15 weeks** — Fast Company called it "the highest-profile maternity leave
  since Marissa Mayer," and coverage framed it explicitly against Mayer's
  back-in-days norm. Her leave-taking was itself a deliberate public act.
- **2016–17:** continued advocacy — CNN (Nov 2016) "wants parents to take
  more time off"; CNN (May 2017) calling on the Trump administration to
  enact federal paid parental leave.
- **2023:** steps down as YouTube CEO; **Aug 2024:** dies at 56.

## The two witnesses, read together

**Where they agree** (and where the archive sides with both): no policy
existed in 1999; the first leave was improvised at founder level; the
founders were personally supportive of the pregnant employee; what got
codified was **12 weeks** — which is exactly what our 2005 corpus snapshot
shows (12 weeks at 75% pay), two years before the 18-week expansion.

**Where they diverge:**

| | Cairns (2026) | Wojcicki (2014–17) |
|---|---|---|
| The founders' support | "remarkable favoritism," naive precedent-setting | founder enlightenment, origin myth of data-driven benefits |
| The generous proposal | "a mere half-year off. Without cash gifts" floated | a modest "little time off" ask, institutionalized as 12 weeks |
| The moral | generosity to "mama" was unfair to non-mothers | generosity to mothers was good for business |
| Register | satire/grievance, book promotion | advocacy op-ed, business case, policy campaign |

**Where the archive complicates both:** the 2005 careers page says 12 weeks
**at 75% pay**. The partial-pay detail appears in *neither* telling —
Cairns's version needs the policy lavish, Wojcicki's needs it foundational;
neither dwells on the fact that Google's "generous" first policy docked a
quarter of a new mother's salary (full pay arrived with the 2007 change, per
Bock's "partial pay to full pay" framing). The archive holds the detail both
narrators drop — the project's thesis in miniature.

**Both are testimony with incentives.** Cairns's incentives are covered in
the main report (memoir promotion, satirical register). Wojcicki's version
is also curated: told in advocacy op-eds, structured as a business case,
anchored on a self-reported statistic, and polished into retrospective
coherence (the children↔milestones mapping is storycraft, however true).
Her account is *closer to the codified record* — the 12-week claim matches
the 2005 snapshot — but it is still a narrative built for a purpose. The
story should treat both symmetrically: two interested witnesses, one
archive.

**One asymmetry to keep:** Wojcicki told her version for a decade while
Cairns stayed silent; Cairns published hers only after Wojcicki's death,
when the other witness could no longer answer. That sequencing is a fact
about the discourse, not a judgment about either account's truth.
