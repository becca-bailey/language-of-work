# Netflix performance-culture — case-study outline

The model that propagated. Centers the 2009 Netflix Culture deck — which Sheryl Sandberg called the most
important document to come out of Silicon Valley — and the thesis that its sports/
performance metaphor launders subjective judgment into false objectivity, and into that
gap bias enters.

**Framing (the one the data supports — not "Netflix took over tech culture"):** Netflix
authored the *canonical language* of the performance-filter culture; its *ethos* spread
across the industry by **convergence**; its *distinctive, brutal formulations* stayed
almost entirely on Netflix's own page — with one striking exception, **Coinbase**.

---

## Act 1 — The deck (what Netflix codified)
The 2009 deck ([data/netflix/canon/culture_deck_2009.md](../data/netflix/canon/culture_deck_2009.md))
defined a performance-filter culture in unusually blunt terms:
- **Talent density** — "Increase Talent Density faster than Complexity Grows."
- **Team, not a family** — "We're like a pro sports team, not a kid's recreational team."
- **The keeper test** — "Which of my people, if they told me they were leaving… would I
  fight hard to keep?"
- **Fire the adequate** — "adequate performance gets a generous severance package."
- And it defines "performance" with **no metric** — "you accomplish amazing amounts of
  important work… focus on great results rather than process."
- *Crucially, the deck concedes the analogy is flawed:* a slide titled **"Pro Sports Team
  Metaphor is Good, but Imperfect."**

## Act 2 — The spread (narrow lift + broad convergence)
From the validated propagation tracker
([data/culture_propagation_findings.md](../data/culture_propagation_findings.md)):
- **The one clean lift — Coinbase.** *"Unremarkable performance gets a generous severance
  package"* (2024) ≈ the deck's *"adequate performance gets a generous severance
  package."* A one-word edit. Coinbase is the disciple.
- **The harsh mechanics stayed Netflix-only** in public copy — keeper test,
  "high performer ≫ average," "not a family." Companies took the ethos, not the
  specifics.
- **The broad vocabulary isn't Netflix's** — "raise the bar" is *Amazon's* Leadership
  Principle; "best and brightest"/"A-players" predate everyone. Convergence, not
  propagation; excluded from the Netflix claim.
- **Netflix invented it — and softened it** (intra-Netflix evolution,
  [data/netflix_evolution.json](../data/netflix_evolution.json)). Netflix's own culture
  copy carried *"adequate performance → a generous severance"* every year **2009–2022**,
  then **dropped it in its ~2023 rewrite** (2024–2026 keep keeper-test / dream-team /
  "high performer" only; verified against the actual 2024+ text, not capture noise).
  Coinbase printed the formula in **2024 — the year Netflix retired it.** Netflix invented
  it, softened it; only the disciple kept the faith.
- **Soft ⇄ hard mapping** (interpretation): companies didn't copy "keeper test," but the
  vaguer phrases do the same filtering work — *"keeper test" / "adequate → severance"* ⇄
  *"we hire only the best" / "raise the bar."* Both make a subjective cut while claiming
  objectivity.

## Act 3 — The false scoreboard (the thesis)
From the objectivity audit
([data/objectivity_audit.md](../data/objectivity_audit.md)): across 1,103 culture chunks,
**7% claim objective performance/merit; 0% define an actual metric.** The smoking gun:
**Netflix explicitly refuses to keep score** — *"We have no bell curves or rankings or
quotas such as 'cut the bottom 10%'"* — while demanding you be a "high performer" judged
by a manager's gut (the keeper test). The loudest invocation of the sports scoreboard is
the one that most explicitly declines to measure.

### The two problems with the sports metaphor (interpretation — see guardrail)
1. **Contextual/temporal.** A basketball career is a short, declining arc with a fixed
   position and the expectation of retirement; knowledge work isn't. "Performance" varies
   with team, manager, project, and life circumstances — it is not a stable individual
   trait, and the 10x-engineer-forever stereotype is a dream, not the median reality.
2. **Unmeasurable (the core).** A basketball player is judged on the *same* hoop, court,
   rules, and scoreboard — an objective standard with a box score. Software "performance"
   has no such standard; productivity metrics (lines of code, story points) are noisy and
   gameable, and the original "10x" finding (Sackman 1968) was methodologically broken.
   The sports metaphor borrows objectivity's *aura* without its *measurability*; the
   actual judgment is discretionary — which is the documented entry point for bias
   (gendered/racialized performance-review language; forced-distribution "stack ranking,"
   widely abandoned, e.g. Microsoft 2013).

## Honesty guardrail
The instrument measures the **spread of the language** and the **objectivity-without-a-
metric gap** — both from careers/culture self-presentation, not practice. **Bias is the
interpretive thesis**, grounded in external literature, *not* a measured output here.
Vocabulary adoption ≠ running the keeper test; timing/precedence ≠ causation; small N.
