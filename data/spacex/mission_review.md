JOB DESCRIPTIONS: Development Engineer: BSME or BSAAE degree with experience in fabrication, assembly and testing of liquid systems and components. Analytical capabilities in fluid dynamics and heat transfer desired. Experience with rocket engine development a plus. Design Engineer: BSME or BSAAE degree with experience in the design of liquid systems and components. Requires proficiency with 3D CAD program, Solidworks experience preferred. Familiarity with liquid rocket fluid systems including valves, seals, fittings, bellows, and other components also desired. Components Engineer: BSME or BSAAE degree with experience in specifying, developing and procuring components for fluid systems. Requires a general knowledge of pneumatic valves, solenoid valves, hydraulic valves, regulators, check valves and other fluid components. Experience with liquid rocket engine and launch vehicle systems, including pressure vessels including metallic and composite tankage desired. Senior Safety Engineer: BSE or higher with experience in flight safety systems and fulfilling the requirements of EWR 127-1 and RCC-319. Structural Analyst: MSE or PhD with experience in analysis of mechanical structures using finite element analysis. Thermal and aerodynamic analysis skills also desired.
- SpaceX is developing a family of launch vehicles intended to reduce the cost and increase the reliability of access to space by approximately an order of magnitude. The company officially began operations in June 2002 and is located in the heart of the aerospace industry in Southern California, about a mile from LAX. Our first launch vehicle, named Falcon, is a mostly reusable rocket capable of placing either 1250 pounds in the standard configuration or 4000 pounds in the heavy configuration into low Earth orbit. We expect to have the Falcon physically ready for launch by late 2003, with the actual liftoff date subject to regulatory approval and other external dependencies. Following this vehicle, SpaceX plans to develop a large three stage rocket using the first and second stages of Falcon as its second and third stages. That vehicle would compete in the heavy lift payload class occupied by Arianespace, Boeing, Lockheed, China Aerospace and Russia’s Krunichev. The standard Falcon is priced at $6 million, which is an 80% reduction relative to the current NASA list price of a Pegasus launch vehicle from Orbital Sciences. Pegasus, at a NASA list price of $30 million, is the lowest cost American launch vehicle now available. Whereas Falcon will be the lowest cost per flight to orbit, our follow on heavy lift rocket will aspire to be the lowest cost per unit mass to orbit. SpaceX is privately developing the entire Falcon rocket from the ground up, including both engines, the turbo-pump, the cryogenic tank structure and the guidance system. A ground up internal development increases difficulty and the required investment, but no other path will achieve the needed improvement in the cost of access to space.

A ground up internal development increases difficulty and the required investment, but no other path will achieve the needed improvement in the cost of access to space.

The site’s location on the southeast coast of the US provides access to a wide range of low and medium inclination orbits frequently used by communications and Earth-observing satellites and by supply missions to the International Space Station.
