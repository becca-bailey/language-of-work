## 2014 (16 chunks)

- **How do we work?** — Avoid meetings Any meeting with more than three people at Coinbase is highly suspect. Meetings are killers of productivity, and if left unchecked can wreak havoc on an organization. Not all meetings are bad at Coinbase; we love one-on-one meetings, presentations where a single person presents to a group, and external meetings with clients. But the standard “n number of people internally discussing a topic” is avoided and broken up when spotted. Currently the only required meeting we have at Coinbase is a once-a-week standup meeting for each team, where each person presents for 60 seconds or less. Headphones in = no interruptions It's easy to get caught up in urgent, reactive tasks each day which prevent you from getting the important things done. Context switching has been shown to be another killer of productivity. When someone has headphones in, this is a signal to the rest of the company that they are “in the zone” and should not be disturbed. Exceptions to this rule include if they are missing a pre-scheduled event on their calendar or if the website is down. Flexible hours, vacation policy, and locations You can work any hours you'd like at Coinbase. There is no set time you need to be in the office, and the vacation policy is quite flexible. If you are more productive at home for a day, you should work there instead. Great collaboration benefits happen from working in the office, but you know best what the right balance is to make. Feedback We strive to have a culture of open feedback. Whether this is a 1-on-1 with your manager each week, transparency at team meetings, or anonymous feedback in peer reviews. We want you to always be comfortable sharing feedback within the organization.
- **How do we work?** — Engineering led Coinbase is an engineering led organization. This means that on the product team, engineers report to other engineers. We hire builders, and this extends all the way to the founders. Coinbase does not hire non-technical product managers. (Of course, many key teams outside of product are not engineering led, including business development, sales, compliance, support, and more). Praise in public, criticize in private When you have feedback for someone on how they could improve, avoid group settings or email threads with multiple people. Praise can happen in public, but feedback should happen in private after you've had some time to think it through. Communication We spend a lot of time in chat and using internal email lists. It allows asynchronous communication which is far more efficient - you can communicate asynchronously with many people at the same time but only one synchronously. This also prevents interruptions when people are focused. More direct communication and collaboration is great for daily shared lunches and dinners.
