## How to read these charts

- **What the axis is:** a direction in embedding space, averaged from
  sixteen word-pair arrows that differ only in gender. "Masculine-coded"
  means computed proximity to that pole in decades of English text — a
  reflection of the training data's associations, never a judgment about
  who a working style belongs to.
- **The voice confound:** assertive founder-manifesto prose codes masculine
  independent of intensity (anti-hustle Basecamp sits near the top), while
  HR-authored benefits copy pulls feminine. Fair comparisons are within a
  register, not across registers.
- **Names are gendered words:** "Eric" scores like "he." Dropping every
  sentence with an explicit gender term, and then also every sentence with
  a likely personal name, reproduces the company ordering almost exactly
  (rank correlations 0.99 and 0.94).
- **No human-rating backstop, on purpose:** asking a judge whether "bias
  for action" sounds masculine would re-import the intuitions the method
  bypasses. Validation is known-answer anchors and split-half reliability.
- **Language, not outcomes:** no promotion rates or pay gaps here. The
  downstream effect is measured elsewhere — in
  [the 2011 job-ads experiments](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0022530), women
  rated masculine-worded ads less appealing and reported lower belonging,
  while their assessment of their own ability to do the job didn't change.
