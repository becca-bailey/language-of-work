## Power-shift cases — when control concentrates, culture hardens overnight

Documented illustrations, not in the quantitative aggregate — the moment ownership or

## How to read it

Across 11 companies, **DEI** — the worker-oriented intervention — tracks the worker-power
cycle: it rises and falls with the quits rate even after the decade's shared upward trend is
removed (first-difference r ≈ +0.58). **Idealism** (the optimism barometer) looks like it
tracks power too (raw r ≈ +0.63, +0.74 smoothed), but strip the trend and most of it fades
(first-difference r ≈ +0.32): it rides the same boom rather than responding to worker
leverage year to year — consistent with workers being cynical of "change the world" talk.
**Performance/intensity** stays flat near the top regardless (r ≈ −0.04 differenced) — it
serves whoever can hire and fire, so it needs no leverage to survive. **Wellbeing/balance**,
the worker concession, is the honest surprise: flat and only weakly co-moving (≈ +0.15
smoothed) even scored over both mission and benefits copy, and the benefits corpus is thin
(≈135 chunks), so it is under-measured here.

## Honesty

All three metrics are means over the same 11 companies, each scored on all three axes — no
per-metric subsetting. Basecamp and Twitter/X are documented as power-shift cases above, not
in this aggregate (Basecamp has no careers corpus; Twitter/X is thin post-2022). The quits
rate is economy-wide, not tech-only — a proxy for worker bargaining power; tech tracked the
national swing. Co-movement, not causation.

The strongest result is also the most robust: DEI's link to worker power survives
first-differencing and leave-one-company-out (range +0.45 to +0.66), and held steady when
Meta's history was substantially re-expanded. Idealism's raw correlation is mostly shared
trend, so read it as co-trending, not power-responsive. At six companies the aggregate was
too noisy to read DEI; it only firmed up at eleven — so treat it as suggestive, not decisive.
(Lag is uninterpretable on a series this short and trending.)
