## How to read these charts

- **DEI language is a signal, not policy** — not proof of conditions for
  marginalized people, but the kind of signal that decides whether someone
  applies.
- **Why a classifier, not a score:** the inclusion embedding axis measures
  topic, not direction — anti-DEI copy ("a refuge from division") scores
  high because it borrows belonging vocabulary. The register classifier is
  what surfaces the flip.
- **"Civilizational mission"** marks chunks where a hiring page frames the
  employer's identity around defending or ensuring the future of "the
  West." The category requires the explicit invocation — generic
  defense-industry copy about deterrence or "the world's most important
  institutions" does not count — and in this corpus every chunk in it is
  Palantir's ("We built Palantir to ensure the future of the West, not to
  tinker at the margins," 2026). The label is descriptive, and the claim
  stays narrow: this vocabulary appeared, on these pages, in these years,
  while the belonging-era vocabulary went away. The vocabulary itself is
  not neutral — "defending Western civilization" is a load-bearing phrase
  in contemporary far-right rhetoric, and also the open thesis of
  Palantir's CEO's book; those readings are not mutually exclusive, and
  [the essay version of this story](https://github.com/becca-bailey/language-of-work/blob/main/posts/stories/dei-essay.md)
  carries the full, cited discussion.
- **Retreat happens by deletion, not announcement:** companies mostly drop
  DEI language by removing and replacing it, which is why the retreat
  shows as shrinking bars and rising apolitical framing rather than
  explicit rollback statements.
