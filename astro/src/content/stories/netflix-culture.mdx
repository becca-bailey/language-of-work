## Act 3 · The false scoreboard — borrowed objectivity, no score

Across 1,103 culture chunks, the language claims objective performance but never defines how
it's measured.

<AuditBar />

<ObjectivityMatrix />

Netflix explicitly refuses to keep score: "We have no bell curves or rankings or quotas such
as 'cut the bottom 10% every year'" — while demanding you be a "high performer" judged by a
manager's gut (the keeper test).

### The soft language does the same work

Companies didn't copy "keeper test," but the vaguer phrases make the same subjective cut
while claiming objectivity (interpretation).

<ImplicitExplicit />

Pervasive objectivity rhetoric, no defined metric. The language borrows sports' objectivity
without its scoreboard — and the discretion that fills the gap is where bias enters.
Netflix's own deck even concedes the analogy is "good, but imperfect."

Measures self-presentation and the missing metric — not bias itself, which is the
interpretive thesis. Vocabulary adoption ≠ running the keeper test; small N.
